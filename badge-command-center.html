Locomotive
HP&F engine "The Bee" — a named, documented locomotive photographed on the Hartford Providence & Fishkill line in the 19th century. Inbound from the Connecticut state line, The Bee worked every stop on the Rhode Island corridor: Greene → Washington → Summit → Anthony → Quidnick → Arctic → River Point → Olneyville → Dike Street → Providence Union Station. Mill towns, factory villages, working Rhode Island from end to end. The western section of this line — abandoned in 1968 — is now known locally as the Trestle Trail, a hiking and biking path through the Coventry and Greene corridor where the tracks once ran. The rails are gone. The geography remembers.

Locomotive
Class I-5 4-6-4 Hudson No. 1400 — semi-streamlined, Art Deco, late 1930s. The locomotive that hauled the Merchants Limited along the Shore Line. Built for speed and presence, the I-5 was the crown of the New Haven's passenger fleet. Level 3 — Rocky Point Rides
Warwick, Rhode Island  ·  Narragansett Bay  ·  Est. 1847  ·  Closed 1995
Rocky Point Park was the second oldest amusement park in the United States — opening just one year after Lake Compounce in Bristol, Connecticut, and more than two decades before Cedar Point. For nearly 150 years it was Rhode Island's most beloved summer destination — shore dinners, clam cakes, and rides that generations of New Englanders never forgot.
These badges represent the actual rides from Rocky Point's later years. Each one tells the story of a ride that once stood on the shores of Narragansett Bay.
Collect all 7 and complete the Rocky Point collection.

Visible for miles in every direction, it was the first thing that felt like the West.

Fajada Butte rises 443 feet from the floor of Chaco Canyon — the center of ancestral Puebloan civilization from 850 to 1150 CE.
